Case study · Relational design + business rules

Database & SQL Project

Selected database coursework demonstrating how I turn business rules into entities, relationships, integrity constraints and queryable SQL structures.

University work · Public summary, assessed artefacts not reproduced

Problem

A database is only useful when its structure reflects the business accurately. Poor entity boundaries, duplicated data or weak integrity rules create reporting and operational problems later.

My role

I worked through conceptual modelling, normalisation, relational design and SQL implementation with an emphasis on connecting database decisions to the business use case.

What I worked on

Model the domain

Identify entities, keys, relationships and cardinality from business requirements before writing queries.

Protect integrity

Use normalisation and relational constraints to reduce duplication and keep stored data consistent.

Make data usable

Write SQL that retrieves and combines data in forms that support operational questions and reporting.

Proof of work

ERD thinkingEntity relationships and identifiers are designed before implementation, rather than treating tables as isolated spreadsheets.
NormalisationStructures are reviewed for duplicated or dependent data and decomposed where needed.
SQL implementationOracle SQL is used to create, populate and query relational structures.
Business connectionSchema decisions are explained in terms of business rules, not only database syntax.
